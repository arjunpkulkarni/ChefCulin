Roasted-duck aroma studies identify odorants including 2-furfurylthiol, dimethyl trisulfide, methional, hexanal, nonanal and 1-octen-3-ol — a fatty, roasty, meaty, sulfurous and earthy register. This is broader cooked-meat chemistry, not unique to duck.

Astringency has direct evidence as a counterweight to oral fat sensation — alternating mild astringent stimuli with fatty foods decreases perceived fattiness. Sourness and salivation sit adjacent to this, but are a separate mechanism.

A note on what's here: ducks are farmed overwhelmingly in Asia, but production is not the same as cookery. Italy, Mexico and Central Europe all have documented duck traditions a chef can use — and a lens built only on where the birds live would miss all of them.

Across documented threads, duck has been made into very different things. These aren't options to pick from — they're states cooks have arrived at, and what each one opens downstream. Commit to one if it's useful, ignore them all if it isn't.
